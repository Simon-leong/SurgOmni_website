Abstract

Laparoscopic videos are often affected by diverse degradations arising at different stages of the imaging process, making all-in-one surgical restoration necessary for real-world deployment. However, advancing all-in-one surgical restoration remains constrained by three fundamental limitations: (1) existing training datasets either lack exact pixel-level degraded-clean correspondence or rely on simplified synthesis that inadequately models the formation of complex surgical degradations; (2) existing real-world testing datasets often remain image-based and cover only a limited range of degradation types and surgical scenarios, restricting comprehensive evaluation of model performance; and (3) the relationship between all-in-one training degradation composition and real-world testing generalization remains poorly understood. To address these limitations, we introduce SurgOmni, a unified dataset and benchmark suite for all-in-one surgical video restoration. Specifically, we first propose OmniSyn, a 44,000-frame paired training dataset comprising 14 degradation configurations and constructed through a formation-aware synthesis pipeline, where scene- and lens-space degradations are rendered via a physics-based 3D engine with temporal dynamics, while exposure- and illumination-related degradations are modeled through exposure integration and content-aware spatial photometric modeling, respectively. Meanwhile, we further propose OmniBench, a 10,000-frame real-world video testing dataset spanning 6 surgical types, 10 target organs, 14 degradation configurations, and 27 surgical scenes. Extensive training-dataset comparisons first show that models trained on OmniSyn consistently achieve the strongest generalization to real surgical data. Comprehensive benchmarking across diverse restoration methods on OmniBench then reveals distinct strengths and limitations in restoration quality and temporal motion deviation that are obscured by limited real-world evaluation. Beyond performance evaluation, we further investigate how training-data degradation composition affects real-world testing generalization, revealing the relative effectiveness of different composition strategies. These findings provide practical guidance for training-data design and restoration method development for all-in-one surgical restoration.

OmniSyn

OmniSyn contains 44,000 paired high-resolution frames in 440 videos, organized into a single-degradation training subset covering 5 degradation types and a multi-degradation training subset covering 9 degradation combinations. It adopts two formation-aware synthesis branches, where scene- and lens-space degradations are generated through physics-based 3D rendering with temporal trajectory dynamics, while exposure- and illumination-related degradations are modeled through code-based synthesis using exposure integration and content-aware spatial photometric modeling.

OmniBench

OmniBench is designed for real-world evaluation under authentic surgical degradations. It contains 10,000 high-resolution frames in 100 videos spanning 6 surgical types, 10 target organs, 14 degradation configurations, and 27 surgical scenes, including 43 single-, 45 dual-, and 12 triple-degradation videos. OmniBench is reserved exclusively for evaluation and is not used for training or model selection.

Discussion: Path Towards All-in-One Surgical Restoration

Our experiments highlight four key observations.

01

All-in-one surgical restoration training datasets should aim to jointly provide accurate degraded-clean correspondence, faithful physical degradation modeling, and diverse coverage of surgical scenes and degradation severities. Accurate correspondence and realistic degradation formation are associated with more consistent task-relevant preservation, while broader scene and severity coverage can provide more transferable supervision and may partially compensate for a smaller training-set scale.

02

All-in-one surgical restoration training composition strategies should prioritize sufficient single-degradation supervision to establish stable restoration anchors under a limited training budget. Individual degradation mechanisms and severity variations are easier to model and cover through synthesis, enabling more reliable restoration capabilities for each degradation type. In contrast, multi-degradation supervision remains constrained by the domain gap between synthetic and real formation mechanisms, motivating more realistic multi-degradation synthesis for interaction modeling.

03

All-in-one surgical restoration methods may benefit from jointly preserving fine-grained structures and limiting restoration-induced motion deviations rather than focusing only on stronger single-frame restoration or additional temporal inputs. Fine-grained structural modeling is associated with better preservation of tissue boundaries and instrument details, while more constrained restoration mappings may reduce unnecessary inter-frame motion deviations.

04

All-in-one surgical restoration methods using generative priors may benefit from incorporating measurement-consistency constraints and avoiding excessive latent-space compression. Without measurement-consistency constraints, generative priors may have greater freedom to re-estimate tissue boundaries and local textures beyond the observed evidence, while strong latent compression may further discard high-resolution texture and motion-relevant information.

Reliable real-world generalization benefits from accurate degraded-clean correspondence, faithful degradation modeling, and effective training composition, while robust restoration requires consideration of both surgical structural preservation and restoration-induced motion deviation.

Conclusion

We introduce SurgOmni, a unified dataset and benchmark suite for all-in-one surgical video restoration, comprising the formation-aware paired training dataset and the real-world video testing dataset.

Extensive experiments show that reliable real-world generalization benefits from accurate degraded-clean correspondence, faithful degradation modeling, and effective training composition, while robust restoration requires consideration of both surgical structural preservation and restoration-induced motion deviation. These findings provide a practical foundation for training-data construction, training composition strategy design, and restoration method development in future all-in-one surgical restoration.
